Measurement for Change · Online workshop

Measurement 101 Workshop

Running live end of January – mid April 2027 · online only

Do you want to understand your organization's impact on your community? This hands-on workshop guides you in developing measures and tools to track your program's impact. You come with information about your program and we help you measure it.

The workshop is open to non-profits globally. Must have significant overlap with Toronto's time zone (UTC−5 in winter, UTC−4 during daylight saving time).

Register for the info session →

How the workshop works

1

Hands-on training

Learn how to decide what to measure, when to collect the data, and how to build good measurement tools.

2

Apply the learnings

Apply what you learn to develop a real-life measurement project your organization can use.

3

1:1 guidance from graduate students

Each organization is paired with a graduate student skilled in measurement who supports you every step of the way.

The details

When

End of January to mid April 2027. The workshop is online only and open to non-profits globally. Must have significant overlap with Toronto's time zone (UTC−5 in winter, UTC−4 during daylight saving time).

Time commitment

Expect 2–3 hours per week for the 12-week duration of the workshop.

Cost

$350 per organization. Register as many staff from your organization as you wish at no additional cost.

What you'll leave with

A measurement framework or tool of your choice. You'll decide what to measure and how to measure it, and build tools for collecting both qualitative and quantitative data to use at your organization.

What past participants said

Measurement 101 first ran as a pilot with fourteen organizations, including mental health, food security, literacy, and community foundation groups. All participants said they would recommend it.

“I'm so grateful for this course — it sharpened my impact measurement skills and shifted my mindset, showing that data isn't just for reporting, but for learning, growth, and real change driven by the voices of those we serve.” — The Food Bank of Waterloo Region
“[This course was] well paced and provided quality content and instruction that was very helpful to our agency and we left with a plan to move forward with.” — Project READ
